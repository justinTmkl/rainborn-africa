# Kefalonia — Keynote Production Spec

The deck is not a travel report. It is a 24-slide argument with one job: by the
last slide the viewer should be thinking *"why aren't we just booking this?"*

Everything below is the craft layer under that argument — typography, colour,
icons, layout, motion, and what to actually photograph. Sicily is never named,
never implied, never compared. The deck only ever talks about what a week here
would feel like.

---

## 1 · Dramaturgy

| Act | Slides | Job | Emotional temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| I · The hook | 1–4 | A question, not a destination. Name the place last. | cool, quiet, curious |
| II · The days | 5–15 | Seven feelings, one per slide. No itinerary. | warm, rising |
| III · The window | 16–19 | Why late September — framed as luck, not logistics | bright, factual, brief |
| IV · The ask | 20–24 | Honesty, then the plan, then the yes | intimate, low, warm |

Two rules that hold the whole thing together:

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs a second thought, it is two slides.
- **The peak is silent.** Slide 11 (Golden hour) has nine words on it. Say
  nothing for ten seconds. That silence is the most persuasive moment in the deck.

## 2 · Typography

Two families, three roles. Nothing else.

| Role | Face | Size | Tracking | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display | **Cambria** (serif) | 38–96 pt | normal | the one statement per slide |
| Body | **Calibri** | 15–19 pt | normal | the single supporting line |
| Kicker | Calibri Bold, caps | 11.5 pt | +4 | chapter label, once per slide |
| Mark | Calibri, caps | 9.5 pt | +2.5 | bottom-left orientation tag |
| Data | Courier New | 13 pt | normal | day abbreviations, numbers |

**Why a serif for display.** Sans-serif headlines read as *product*. A serif at
large size reads as *invitation* — it carries warmth that Helvetica cannot. The
body stays sans so the two never compete.

**Substitution note.** Cambria and Calibri ship with Office on Windows and Mac,
so the file renders identically on any machine you present from. If you swap in
webfonts (Cormorant Garamond + DM Sans are the design-system equivalents), embed
them — a missing display face collapses the entire deck.

Hard limits, enforced across all 24 slides:

- Display never exceeds two lines.
- Body never exceeds two lines.
- Never more than **one** kicker and **one** body block per slide.
- Word count per slide: **≤ 25**. Most slides sit at 8–14.

## 3 · Colour

```
ink        #12303B   display type on light grounds
inkSoft    #3E5A66   secondary type on light
inkMute    #77909B   marks, captions, de-emphasised rows
cream      #FBF6EE   the light ground
white      #FFFFFF   display type on dark grounds
gold       #F0B849   kickers on dark — the accent that means "pay attention"
goldSoft   #FFDFA0   the humour line, the aside, the warm whisper
sea        #0E7A8C   mid-ground
seaDeep    #04222F   the darkest ground
clay       #C0714C   kickers on light — the second accent
ember      #4A2A18   the only type colour that survives the sunset glow
markLight  #A6BDC6   white knocked back, pre-blended for dark photos
```

**Rules of use**

- **One accent per slide.** Gold on dark grounds, clay on light. Never both.
- **Grounds alternate.** Dark → light → dark across the acts, so each slide
  arrives as a change of light. Never three of the same ground in a row.
- **Gold is rationed.** It appears on 9 of 24 slides. If it were on all of them
  it would stop meaning anything.
- **Never put white type in a bright glow.** Slide 11's type is `ember`, not
  white, precisely because it sits in the brightest part of the sunset.

**A pitfall worth naming:** a cool teal scrim over a warm sunset image turns
brown. If you need to darken a golden frame, darken it with a warm scrim
(`mavrodaphne` / deep clay), never with the sea colours.

## 4 · Layout

Four layouts. Every slide is one of them.

**A · Full-bleed statement** (slides 1, 3, 9, 12, 16, 21)
Image edge to edge. Bottom scrim. Statement at 60–75 % height, flush left at the
1" margin. Body line directly under it. Mark bottom-left.
*Use when the image is the argument.*

**B · Centred hold** (4, 11, 14, 23)
One centred line, nothing else. No kicker, no mark on 11 and 23.
*Use for the three or four moments that should stop the room.*

**C · Left-column editorial** (2, 6, 10, 13, 20, 22)
Kicker → statement → hairline → body, all on the left third to half. The right
half stays empty. That emptiness is doing work — it is what makes the deck read
as considered rather than crammed.

**D · Structured grid** (5, 15, 17, 18, 19)
Cards, stats, quote pairs, or the day plan. Maximum seven items. Everything
aligns to the same left margin as the layouts around it.

**Geometry, fixed across the deck**

- Outer margin: **1.0"** on a 13.333 × 7.5" stage. Nothing crosses it.
- Statement baseline: 2.2–2.6" (upper third) or 4.6–5.6" (lower third). Never
  dead centre except in Layout B.
- Hairline rule: 1.1" wide, 0.028" tall, used only to separate a claim from its
  consequence (slides 13, 20).
- Gutter between paired images: 0.33".
- Labels sit **flush with the left edge of the image they name**, never indented.

## 5 · Icons

Line only. 1.6 px stroke on a 48-unit grid, round caps and joins, no fill, no
colour. Drawn at 240 px and placed at 0.32–0.5".

| Icon | Where | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| coffee cup | ch. Morning coffee | slowness |
| double wave | ch. Hidden beaches | water |
| sailboat | ch. Boat day | freedom |
| sun with rays | ch. Golden hour | the peak |
| crescent moon | ch. Slow evenings | evening |
| clock | ch. No plans / logistics | time you own |
| heart | ch. Only us | the actual point |
| thermometer | stats | warmth |
| two figures | stats | crowds |
| paper plane | the ask | the flight |
| euro | the ask | the price |

**Two things that matter more than they sound.** Every icon exists in a light
and a dark variant (`-d` suffix) so it can sit on either ground at full strength
— never dim an icon with transparency to make it fit, redraw it in the right
colour. And a shape that could be read as an arrow is not an aeroplane: the
paper plane needs its fold line, or it becomes a play button.

Icons appear on **11 of 24 slides**. They label chapters and data. They never
decorate.

## 6 · Image composition

Ten photographs carry the deck. Each placeholder frame in the file names its slot.

| Slot | Slide | Shoot for |
|---|---|---|
| water-full | 3 | Water from above, no horizon, no people. Abstraction, not a postcard. |
| dawn | 6 | Shutters half-open from *inside* a room, light falling on an unmade bed. First-person, never a hotel listing. |
| coffee | 7 | Table top, two cups, one hand. Faces cropped out. |
| beach A / B | 8 | Two turquoise bays, near-identical framing — the quiz only works if you genuinely cannot tell them apart. |
| beach-empty | 9 | A cove with no one in it and no visible road. Emptiness is the subject. |
| boat | 10 | Small rental boat, low angle from the water line, wake visible. |
| golden | 11 | Sun 10–15 minutes off the horizon. Warm, huge, uncluttered, no silhouettes in the lower third — the type goes there. |
| dusk | 12 | A taverna table after dinner. Plates unclear, glasses half-full, no faces. |
| night | 14, 23 | Deep blue, almost nothing legible. Enough darkness for centred white type. |
| golden-hold | 24 | Same sunset family as 11, framed with the lower-left quiet — this slide stays up while you talk. |

**Composition rules, all ten**

1. **Negative space where the type goes.** Every image needs a quiet third —
   sky, water, or shadow — for the statement to sit in.
2. **Never a face.** Hands, backs, silhouettes. The moment a stranger's face
   appears, the viewer stops imagining themselves in the frame.
3. **Horizontal only.** Everything is full-bleed 16:9 or a 16:9 crop.
4. **Warm-cool alternation.** Cool water → warm interior → cool water. It makes
   the deck breathe.
5. **No signage, no logos, no dated cars, no crowds.**
6. **Slightly underexposed beats slightly overexposed.** White type needs
   somewhere to live, and blown highlights cannot be recovered.
7. **Minimum 2400 px wide.** A soft image on a projector destroys the premium
   read faster than any typography mistake.

## 7 · Transitions and animation

The single biggest risk to this deck is over-animating it. Apple keynotes use
roughly three effects. So does this.

**Slide transitions**

| Between | Effect | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Every slide, by default | **Fade** (through black on act breaks, direct otherwise) | 0.6 s |
| 3 → 4 (the name reveal) | Fade | 1.2 s — deliberately slower |
| 10 → 11 (into golden hour) | Fade | 1.5 s, then hold |
| 23 → 24 | Fade | 0.8 s |
| Within an act | Fade | 0.4 s |

Set once via *Transitions → Apply to All*, then override those four.

**On-slide builds** — only four, and only where the argument needs a beat:

- Slide 5, the seven cards: appear **one by one, 0.15 s apart, Fade only**. It
  should feel like a list being counted, not like objects flying in.
- Slide 8, the quiz answer: the reveal line appears on click, after the guess.
- Slide 17, the four stats: appear together, not sequentially.
- Slide 19, the two empty days: the accent rows fade in last.

**Ken Burns.** If you present from the HTML deck instead of PowerPoint, the
full-bleed images drift at **1.0 → 1.06 scale over 18 s, ease-out**. Slow enough
that nobody consciously notices — which is the entire point. Do not add it in
PowerPoint; PowerPoint's implementation stutters.

**Banned outright:** fly-in, spin, bounce, checkerboard, cube, morph on type,
sound effects, any transition with a direction. If an effect draws attention to
itself, it is telling the viewer they are watching a presentation. They should
feel like they are looking at a place.

**Pacing.** 24 slides in 6–7 minutes. Roughly 15 s per slide, except:
slide 4 (10 s of nothing), slide 11 (silence, 10 s), slide 23 (hold until she
answers — do not fill it).

## 8 · Psychology, slide by slide

The techniques, stated plainly so you can defend any of them:

- **1–2, question first.** Nobody argues with a question. Opening on "Can I show
  you something?" makes her a participant instead of an audience.
- **4, name it late.** The place is unnamed for three slides. Curiosity is doing
  the work that a title slide would have wasted.
- **5, feelings not activities.** An itinerary invites comparison. A feeling
  cannot be compared against anything.
- **6, second person.** "Imagine waking up here" puts her in the room. Every
  other slide is "we".
- **8, the quiz.** She commits to a guess, then learns it was the same place
  twice. Small, harmless, and it makes the water memorable.
- **10, the joke.** "Captain: whoever is less hungover." Humour lowers defences
  right before the emotional peak.
- **14, the real argument.** "We have not had seven uninterrupted days since
  2023." This is the only slide that is actually about the two of you. It lands
  because everything before it earned the right to say it.
- **17, numbers late.** Facts arrive in Act III, after she already wants it.
  Facts before desire are just an argument.
- **20, concede something.** "It is a small island. There is no big city." An
  admitted weakness makes every other claim credible.
- **21, remove the pressure.** "If you would rather go somewhere else — then we
  go somewhere else." Freely given permission to say no is what makes yes real.
- **22, make it small.** Dates, hours, price. A concrete, modest ask.
- **23, ask, then stop talking.** "Shall we?" and silence.
- **24, no thank-you slide.** A thank-you closes a conversation. This one has to
  stay open.

## 9 · Build

```
node make-assets.js      # gradients, scrims, frames, icons, island outline
node build.js            # writes Kefalonia-Keynote.pptx (24 slides)
```

Every slide carries German presenter notes — what to say, and more often, when
to say nothing.

**Two rendering traps found the hard way**, worth knowing before you edit:

- Letter-spaced text that also carries a `transparency` value gets clipped
  mid-glyph on PDF export — the transparency group is measured without the
  spacing. Bake the dimming into the colour instead (`markLight`, `ember`).
- Text boxes need roughly 1.5× the width you would expect when `charSpacing` is
  set, or the last word wraps invisibly.

**Before presenting:** replace the ten placeholder frames with real photographs,
re-check the price on slide 22, and confirm the dates. The frames are designed
to look intentional if you demo the deck as-is, but they are placeholders.
